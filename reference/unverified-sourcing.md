# Unverified sourcing — the mandatory banner discipline

For any situation where a source couldn't be read directly — a fetch tool blocked, a page paywalled, access denied — and findings had to be reconstructed from a secondary summary instead. Read directly from `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/research/water-fasting-ultimate-human-scout.md`, a real file where this happened on an entire research job.

## This is mandatory disclosure, not an edge case

A blocked tool or an unreadable primary source is a normal, expected event, not a failure to hide. The real trail's own instinct, when WebFetch was permission-blocked for an entire job, was to say so immediately, plainly, and at the very top of the file — before any finding. That's the standard: **disclosure comes first, findings come after**, so no reader can reach a finding without first knowing how solid its sourcing actually is.

## The banner — what it must state

From the real trail, verbatim in shape:

> **SOURCING CONFIDENCE — READ FIRST.** [Tool] was **[blocked/unavailable]** for this [whole job/this source]. I could not open [the specific pages] directly. **Every [quote/figure] below is reconstructed from [WebSearch digests / a secondary summary]**, not from reading the primary source. [State any pattern that raises or lowers confidence — e.g., consistency across independent queries.] **[Specific things that need verification before use — exact wording, exact numbers, pricing, etc.] should be verified against the primary sources before anything goes in the book or into a marketing claim.** Per-finding confidence is tagged inline.

Four required elements: **what tool/access failed**, **what wasn't read directly**, **where the findings actually came from instead**, and **an explicit instruction about what needs verification before use**.

## Per-finding confidence tags — every downstream claim, not just the file overall

A file-level banner isn't enough on its own — every individual finding built on unverified sourcing needs its own inline confidence note, because different findings within the same file can have different confidence even under the same blocked-tool constraint. The real trail's pattern: `(MODERATE confidence — consistent across digests; phrase appears in summaries, but I could not read the exact transcript line.)` attached directly to the specific claim it qualifies, not just asserted once at the top and assumed to cover everything below.

Confidence isn't binary. The real trail distinguishes: **STRONG** even under blocked-primary-access, when a fact is corroborated by something independently confirmable (e.g., a URL that itself surfaced in the digest, confirming a page exists); **MODERATE**, when digests are consistent but the exact wording or number can't be confirmed; **SPECULATIVE**, when only one weak signal supports a claim. Tag each finding at the level its actual sourcing supports — don't let the file's overall "mostly consistent" impression round every individual claim up to the same confidence.

## What "verify before use" actually means downstream

An unverified figure isn't unusable — it's usable for internal planning and further investigation, but not for print or a public claim until it clears verification. The real trail states this explicitly: *"exact numbers... should be verified against the primary sources before anything goes in the book or into a marketing claim."* This is the handoff instruction other skills need to respect — `book-ghostwriting-skill` shouldn't draft a specific unverified number into prose, and `Book-to-Content Repurposing` shouldn't caption an unverified figure as settled fact. The banner travels with the finding until it's actually cleared.

## Worked reference — one real blocked-tool situation, handled honestly

The real trail needed to establish who claims to have taken "thousands of people" through water fasting, but WebFetch was blocked for the entire job — every primary page (transcript, landing pages) was unreachable. Rather than guessing or silently treating search-result summaries as equivalent to the primary source, the file opens with the sourcing-confidence banner, attributes the specific claim to the specific person it's about (not to a co-host, not to a third party) with an inline `(MODERATE confidence)` tag, and closes with a sources section explicitly labeled *"all accessed via WebSearch digests; WebFetch blocked — primary pages NOT read."* Nothing in the file pretends to a confidence level the sourcing didn't earn, and the explicit instruction to verify before quoting travels with every number that came out of it.
