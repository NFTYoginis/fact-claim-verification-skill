# Safety & contraindications file

A dedicated artifact, separate from general research clusters, for any book whose subject carries real physical risk for some readers. Read directly from `GabeYoga-HQ/Detox Book/research/08-safety-and-contraindications.md`, a real, complete safety pass.

## Why this is its own file, not folded into general research

The real trail's own standing rule: *"medical risk is not softened here... This is the reader-protection file; it should be over-cautious by design."* General research clusters tier claims about what a practice does. A safety file tiers something different and higher-stakes: who should not do it at all, who needs supervision, and what the actual danger signs are. Mixing this into a general topic cluster risks it reading as one finding among many, softened by proximity to more optimistic material. As its own file, it can't be diluted.

## The two-tier contraindication scale

- **ABSOLUTE** — the consensus of reputable guidance is "do not," not "be careful." The real trail's own framing note: *"these are not 'ask your doctor first' cases — they are 'this book does not recommend you do this' cases. The honest move is to name them as hard stops."* Real examples from the trail: pregnancy/breastfeeding, type 1 diabetes, active or past eating disorder, significant underweight, children.
- **RELATIVE / SUPERVISION-REQUIRED** — possible for some people, but only screened, dose-adjusted, and monitored by a clinician. Not self-directed. Real examples: type 2 diabetes on glucose-lowering medication, any daily prescription medication, older/frail adults, specific cardiac or renal risk factors, and — notably — duration itself: a real threshold like "beyond ~72 hours" can cross from self-directed into supervision-required regardless of the person's underlying health.

Never present a RELATIVE case as if it were a simple ABSOLUTE "no," and never soften an ABSOLUTE case into RELATIVE language ("check with your doctor first") that implies it's a matter of screening rather than a hard stop. See `rules.md`'s Never section.

## Name the single most dangerous moment explicitly, if there is one

A safety file's job isn't just to list contraindications — it's to find and name the specific point of highest real risk, even when it isn't the obvious one. The real trail's own worked example: for water/extended fasting, the most dangerous moment is often not the fast itself but the **refeeding** — reintroducing food too fast after a prolonged fast can trigger refeeding syndrome, a potentially fatal collapse of electrolytes. This is exactly the kind of finding a reader wouldn't expect and a general research cluster might not surface, because it isn't a claim about the practice's benefit — it's a claim about how the practice actually goes wrong. State it as plainly as the trail does: *"a prolonged fast doesn't 'end' when you eat — the refeeding window is part of the protocol, and rushing it is where serious harm happens."*

## Warning signs — stated as a checkable list

Give the reader a concrete, checkable list of "stop and seek care" signs, not a vague "listen to your body." The real trail's list (chest pain, persistent palpitations, severe dizziness/fainting, confusion, persistent vomiting, a specific glucose threshold, extreme weakness) is checkable precisely because it's specific — a reader can compare their actual symptom against the list, rather than having to self-diagnose from a general caution.

## Supervision thresholds — name the actual number

Where the evidence supports a specific threshold (a duration, a lab-value cutoff), state it, cited. The real trail: 24–72 hours generally doesn't require medical supervision for a healthy adult with no contraindications; beyond ~72 hours does. A vague "longer fasts need more caution" is weaker and less useful than a stated, cited threshold a reader can actually apply.

## Every finding in this file still gets tiered and sourced

The safety file is not exempt from `reference/tier-taxonomy.md` — every contraindication, threshold, and warning sign still needs a citation, the same as any other claim. "Over-cautious by design" means the file leans toward naming more risks, not toward asserting risks without evidence. An unsourced caution in a safety file is still a problem; it just fails in the direction of over-restriction rather than overclaim.
