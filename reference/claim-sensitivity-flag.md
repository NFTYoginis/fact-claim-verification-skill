# Claim-sensitivity flag — the extraction-to-revision handoff

This skill sits between two stages of `book-ghostwriting-skill` and has to carry a flag across both boundaries without losing it. Read directly from `book-ghostwriting-skill/reference/stage-extraction.md` (the flag's origin) and this skill's own handoff to Stage 5 (revision).

## Where the flag starts — extraction (Stage 2)

At extraction, before any book structure is imposed, the material index flags anything touching high-risk territory — medical claims, legal claims — for a later safety/honesty pass. The real precedent, from `stage-extraction.md`: *"a claim-sensitivity flag for anything touching high-risk territory (medical claims, legal claims) that needs a safety/honesty pass later... Real precedent flags cancer-and-fasting statements this way: not cut, but flagged for careful framing so the finished chapter never reads as a cure claim."*

**The critical discipline at this stage: flag, don't resolve.** Extraction's job is to notice and mark — not to decide how the claim should ultimately be framed, tiered, or whether it survives at all. Resolving the framing at extraction risks a decision made before the actual evidence has been checked.

## Where the flag lands — this skill

A flag arriving from extraction is an unresolved question, not a completed one. This skill's job on any flagged claim:

1. **Locate the actual claim** the flag is attached to — precisely, not just the general topic area.
2. **Tier it** using `reference/tier-taxonomy.md` — claim-by-claim, same as any other factual claim.
3. **Check it against the dedicated safety file**, if the topic has one (`reference/safety-contraindications.md`) — a flagged claim about a specific practice may directly intersect a named contraindication or supervision threshold.
4. **Resolve the framing explicitly** — state what the manuscript can honestly say about this claim, at what tier, with what caveats, and hand that resolution forward. A flag that arrives here and leaves untouched (still just "flagged," no resolution) has failed the handoff.

## Where the flag goes next — revision (Stage 5)

A resolved claim-sensitivity flag hands forward to `book-ghostwriting-skill` Stage 5 (named revision passes) as a checkable item: does the actual prose, as drafted, match the tier and framing this skill resolved? A chapter that drifts back toward an unsupported claim during a later prose revision — even after the underlying research was correctly tiered — is exactly the failure mode this end-to-end trace exists to catch. The flag isn't fully discharged until revision confirms the shipped prose matches the resolved tier, not just until this skill produces a tiered research file.

## The full chain, traced

**Extraction** (notices, marks, doesn't resolve) → **this skill** (tiers the specific claim, checks it against safety findings if relevant, resolves the framing) → **revision** (confirms the actual prose matches the resolved framing) → **Book-to-Content Repurposing** (any derived marketing claim inherits the same tier, never a looser one).

A flag that breaks anywhere in this chain — noticed but never tiered, tiered but never checked against revision, or resolved here but loosened again in a repurposed caption — is a real failure of the discipline, not a cosmetic gap. Treat "was this flag actually carried all the way through" as a checkable fact on every manuscript, not an assumption.
